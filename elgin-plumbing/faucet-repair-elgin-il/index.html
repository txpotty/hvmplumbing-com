    Simple cartridge swaps can be a DIY job if you identify the correct part. Stuck cartridges, corroded valve bodies and leaks under the sink are best handled by a plumber.

    The most common signs include a persistent drip or leak, difficulty turning the handles, inconsistent water temperature, reduced water pressure from the faucet, or strange noises when operating it. Any of these could indicate internal component wear or damage requiring attention.

    Generally, simple faucet repairs like replacing internal components, handles, or the entire faucet (if it's a like-for-like replacement) do not require a permit in Elgin.     Homes supplied by lake water tend to have moderate water, while most of Illinois relies on groundwater that runs hard to very hard. Over time, these minerals can build up inside your faucets, corroding internal parts, clogging aerators, and stiffening moving components. This often leads to leaks, reduced water pressure, and a shorter faucet lifespan, making regular maintenance or timely repair important.

    Low pressure at one faucet is typically a clogged aerator or cartridge. Low pressure everywhere could mean a failing pressure regulator, a supply issue or old corroded pipes.

    While a minor drip isn't typically an immediate emergency, it's a problem that should be addressed quickly to prevent water waste and potential damage.
