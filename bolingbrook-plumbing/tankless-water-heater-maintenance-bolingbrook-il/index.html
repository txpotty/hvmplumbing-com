    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in Bolingbrook

    The cost for tankless water heater maintenance in Bolingbrook, IL typically ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's age, condition and the extent of descaling required. This includes a full flush and inspection of critical components. More complex issues or additional repairs would be quoted separately.

    Common signs include reduced hot water flow, fluctuating water temperatures, strange noises coming from the unit, or error codes displayed on the control panel. If you notice any of these, it's a strong indication that your tankless water heater in Bolingbrook is due for professional maintenance.

    The descaling process involves shutting off the unit, isolating the water lines and connecting a circulating pump to flush a mild acidic solution through the heat exchanger. This solution dissolves mineral deposits like calcium and magnesium, which are common in hard water, improving heat transfer efficiency.

    A standard tankless water heater maintenance service, including flushing and inspection, usually takes 1 to 2 hours. The exact time can vary depending on the unit's condition, the amount of scale buildup and any unexpected issues encountered during the service.

    Most tankless water heater manufacturers recommend annual maintenance to support good performance and extend the unit's lifespan. In areas with harder water, more frequent checks, perhaps every 6 to 9 months, might help to prevent excessive scale buildup.

    Usually, a permit is not required for routine tankless water heater maintenance, such as flushing and inspections. However, if major repairs or component replacements that alter the original installation are needed, a plumbing permit from Bolingbrook may be necessary to ensure compliance with local building codes.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to several problems, including reduced heating efficiency, higher utility bills, decreased hot water output and eventually, costly breakdowns. Mineral buildup can severely restrict water flow and damage the heat exchanger, potentially leading to premature unit failure and expensive repairs or replacement.
