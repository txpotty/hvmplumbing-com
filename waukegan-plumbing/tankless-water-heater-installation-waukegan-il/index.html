    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Installation in Waukegan

    Most gas tankless installations cost $3,000 to $6,500 including the unit, labor, venting and common gas line upgrades. Electric units can be less, though panel upgrades can add $1,000 to $3,000.

    We install leading tankless water heater brands known for their reliability and efficiency, including Rinnai, Navien, Noritz and Rheem. Our plumbers are trained and experienced with the particular requirements of each manufacturer to deliver strong performance.

    While it may seem appealing, tankless water heater installation is complex and should only be performed by a licensed professional. It involves gas lines, high-voltage electricity, water connections and detailed venting systems, all of which require specialized knowledge and adherence to safety codes. Incorrect installation can be dangerous and void warranties.

    Most tankless water heaters come with a manufacturer's warranty of 10 to 15 years on the heat exchanger and 3 to 5 years on parts. HVM Plumbing also provides a warranty on our installation labor, which we will spell out in your service agreement.

    A straight replacement takes four to six hours. Tank to tankless conversions with gas and venting changes typically take a full day.

    Absolutely. When properly sized, a tankless water heater can provide an endless supply of hot water, even for large families. It is important to select a unit with a high enough GPM (gallons per minute) rating to meet your peak demand.

    Yes, tankless water heaters are very energy efficient in almost any climate. A long heating season keeps boilers and water heaters running hard, and humid summers can cause sweating pipes and damp basements. Since they only heat water on demand, they avoid the standby heat loss common with traditional tanks, which can be substantial even in milder seasons.

    Tankless water heaters offer several key benefits: endless hot water on demand, real energy savings from heating water only when needed, a longer lifespan (often 20+ years) and a compact, space-saving design. They also reduce the risk of tank leaks and the water damage that comes with them.
