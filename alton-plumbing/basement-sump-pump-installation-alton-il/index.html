    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Alton

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which saves wear on the motor. Frozen pipes are a real winter risk in Illinois, especially in exterior walls, unheated garages, and crawlspaces during deep cold snaps. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the discharge.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Alton

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    We routinely install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will match the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less noticeable. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and often more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for maximum protection.

    It is recommended to test your sump pump every 2 to 3 months, especially before and after the rainy season. Simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to confirm the pump activates, drains the water and shuts off properly. Regular testing helps catch issues early.

    Replacing a pump usually does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they are needed.

    Illinois has its own statewide plumbing code administered by the state health department, and some larger cities enforce their own codes as well. In many areas, a plumbing permit is required for new sump pump installations or significant replacements so the work meets local building codes. HVM Plumbing is familiar with local permitting requirements in Alton and can help you obtain the necessary permits so your installation is compliant.
