    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Champaign, IL

    Calcium scale is the leading cause of tankless heater failure. Hard water across much of Illinois leaves mineral scale in water heaters, faucets, and appliances, so softeners are a common upgrade. Scale coats the heat exchanger, reducing efficiency and triggering error codes. We install isolation valves so the unit can be flushed with a vinegar or descaling solution once a year. A scale inhibitor cartridge or whole-home softener provides added protection.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Installation in Champaign

    Most gas tankless installations cost $3,000 to $6,500 including the unit, labor, venting and typical gas line upgrades. Electric units can be less, though panel upgrades can add $1,000 to $3,000.

    We install leading tankless water heater brands known for their reliability and efficiency, including Rinnai, Navien, Noritz and Rheem. Our plumbers are trained and experienced with the specific requirements of each manufacturer to deliver strong performance.

    In Illinois, plumbing and often gas or electrical permits are usually required for tankless water heater installation. HVM Plumbing handles all necessary permit applications and makes sure your installation passes all required inspections by local authorities.

    While it may seem appealing, tankless water heater installation is complex and should only be performed by a licensed professional. It involves gas lines, high-voltage electricity, water connections and intricate venting systems, all of which require specialized knowledge and adherence to safety codes. Incorrect installation can be dangerous and cancel warranties.

    Tankless units eliminate standby heat loss, which can reduce water heating energy use. Savings vary based on household size and usage patterns.

    A straight replacement takes four to six hours. Tank to tankless conversions with gas and venting changes typically take a full day.

    With annual descaling, tankless units often last 15 to 20 years. Skipping maintenance in hard water areas can shorten that considerably.

    Signs you might need a new water heater, tankless or otherwise, include a lack of hot water, rust-colored water, strange noises from the unit or frequent leaks. For tankless systems in particular, frequent error codes or a noticeable drop in hot water flow could point to a need for service or replacement.
