    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Champaign

    The cost of underground pipe repair in Champaign, IL varies a lot depending on the pipe's location, the extent of the damage and the repair method used. Simple repairs might range from $500 to $2,500, while extensive trenchless sewer line replacements could be $5,000 to $15,000 or more. We provide a detailed estimate after a thorough inspection.

    HVM Plumbing is equipped to repair a wide range of underground pipe materials, including PVC, ABS, cast iron, copper, PEX and even older materials like clay or polybutylene. Our technicians are trained on the right repair techniques for each material.

    HVM Plumbing uses high-quality, industry-approved materials from trusted brands known for durability and longevity. This includes PEX piping from manufacturers like Uponor or Viega, and various PVC and ABS pipe products that meet ASTM standards.

    For water lines we typically use PEX or Type K copper, both approved for direct burial. For sewer lines we use Schedule 40 PVC. The right choice depends on the existing pipe and local code.

    Trenchless pipe repair offers several key advantages, including minimal disruption to your yard, landscaping and concrete, which can save on restoration costs. It's usually faster than traditional excavation and less invasive, preserving the look and value of your property. This method is also durable, frequently extending the lifespan of your pipes.

    In most cities, the homeowner owns and maintains the water line from the meter to the house. The utility is usually responsible for the meter and the line on the street side. We can help confirm which side the leak is on.

    Small repairs often do not need a permit, but full water service or sewer line replacement usually does. Work in the public right-of-way always requires coordination with the city. We handle permits when needed.

    Common signs include a water bill that jumps without explanation, a meter that moves with all water off, low pressure and wet or sinking spots in the yard. A hissing sound near the meter box can also signal a leak.
