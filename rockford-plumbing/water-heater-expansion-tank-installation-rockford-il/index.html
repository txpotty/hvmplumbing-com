    Brands and Tank Materials in Rockford, IL

    We install potable-rated thermal expansion tanks from brands such as Watts, Amtrol and A.O. Smith. Potable tanks use a butyl diaphragm and a lined interior so water never touches bare steel. Hard water across much of Illinois leaves scale in water heaters, faucets, and appliances, so softeners are a common upgrade. Stainless steel connections resist corrosion from hard or aggressive water. We avoid tanks designed only for hydronic heating, since they are not approved for drinking water.

    New Expansion Tank Installation in Rockford

    If your home has a pressure reducing valve and no expansion tank, the water heater is probably seeing a pressure spike every heating cycle. We install a new tank on the cold water supply near the heater using a tee and short nipple. The air side is precharged to match your static pressure, usually between 50 and 70 psi. We then run the heater and watch the gauge to confirm pressure stays steady.

    Expansion Tank Sizing for Large Water Heaters in Rockford

    A standard 2 gallon tank works for many 40 and 50 gallon heaters at moderate pressure. Larger heaters, higher incoming pressure or high-capacity systems may need a 4.5 gallon tank or larger. We use manufacturer sizing charts that factor in heater volume, supply pressure and temperature setting. Correct sizing keeps the tank from over-flexing and failing early.

    Replacing Old Expansion Tanks in Rockford

    Expansion tanks do not last forever, and an old, waterlogged or corroded tank offers no protection. If your existing tank in Rockford is showing signs of failure, such as a dripping relief valve or fluctuating water pressure, it is time for a new one. We carefully remove the old unit, dispose of it responsibly and install a new, correctly sized expansion tank. This proactive replacement prevents costly damage to your water heater and plumbing system and restores proper pressure control.

    Expansion Tank Installation Cost Comparison in Rockford, IL

    Adding an expansion tank during a water heater replacement is the most cost-effective approach because the water is already off and the piping is open. Installing one as a standalone job costs a bit more in labor but still pays for itself by protecting the heater. Larger tanks, attic installs and PRV replacement add to the total.
