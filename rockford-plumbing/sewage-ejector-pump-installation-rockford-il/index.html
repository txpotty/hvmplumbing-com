    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Rockford

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so toilets, showers and sinks drain into a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We excavate the floor, set a basin typically 30 to 50 gallons and connect each fixture drain. A solids-handling pump lifts waste up and into the main drain line. The basin lid is sealed and vented to keep odors from escaping.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Rockford

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Rockford

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin usually costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work usually ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    A well-maintained sewage ejector pump usually has a lifespan of 7 to 10 years, though some higher-quality units can last longer. Factors like frequency of use, quality of installation and the type of waste being pumped can affect its longevity. Regular maintenance can help extend its operating life.

    Most ejector pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. Flushing wipes or other non-flushable items can shorten that life significantly.

    Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products, grease and cat litter. These items clog pumps and cause early failure.

    A sump pump removes groundwater or seepage, while an ejector pump handles sewage and wastewater from fixtures. Ejector pumps are designed to pass solids and need a sealed, vented basin.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation typically takes one to two days. This includes excavating the pit, setting the basin and pump, connecting the plumbing and completing the electrical hookups. More complex installations, like those requiring significant trenching or electrical panel upgrades, may take longer.

    Constant running, a sounding alarm, sewage odors, gurgling drains and slow drainage are typical signs. Act quickly to avoid a backup.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area.
