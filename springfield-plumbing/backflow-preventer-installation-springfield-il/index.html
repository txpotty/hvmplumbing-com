    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Springfield, IL

    For low-hazard applications, such as lawn irrigation systems in Springfield, IL, a pressure vacuum breaker, or PVB, is a popular and cost-effective backflow prevention device. PVBs protect against back-siphonage by letting air into the system when pressure drops. Our certified plumbers expertly install PVBs above the highest point of downstream piping, as code requires, to maintain effective protection. We also make sure of proper clearance and drainage.

    Backflow Prevention for Irrigation Systems in Springfield, IL

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Device Installation in Springfield, IL

    For high-hazard applications in Springfield, IL where toxic substances could contaminate the water supply, a reduced pressure zone, or RPZ, device is usually required. RPZ backflow preventers offer the highest level of protection, using a relief valve to discharge water if the pressure drops. HVM Plumbing specializes in RPZ installation, making sure all components are correctly sized, positioned and set up according to state and local plumbing codes. Proper installation is critical for effective operation.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Springfield

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Springfield, IL usually ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation. A basic residential PVB for an irrigation system is generally on the lower end, while a commercial-grade RPZ assembly costs more because of material and labor requirements.

    We install all common types of backflow preventers, including reduced pressure zone assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and double check valve assemblies. Our team will recommend the right device based on your property's hazard level and local plumbing codes in Springfield.

    Most residential backflow preventer installations can be completed within 2 to 4 hours. More complex commercial installations or those requiring extensive pipe changes might take a full day. We always aim for prompt service with minimal disruption to your water supply.

    Most water providers require testable assemblies to be tested at installation and then every year. Some high hazard facilities may be tested more often.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This confirms the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also help with scheduling these annual tests.

    Backflow preventers are installed at cross-connection points where non-potable water could enter the potable water supply. Common locations include irrigation system connections, fire sprinkler lines, boiler systems and connections to auxiliary water supplies, often just after the main water meter or at the point of hazard.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for new backflow assemblies, especially on commercial properties. We check local rules and pull the permit when required.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and boilers.
