    A whole-house water softener system is installed directly on your main water line, treating all the water entering your Springfield home.     A properly sized and installed water softener should not noticeably affect your water pressure. If you experience a noticeable drop in pressure after installation, it could point to an issue with the system or installation, which our plumbers can diagnose and resolve.

    While some homeowners attempt DIY installation, it's usually recommended to hire a professional. Incorrect installation can lead to leaks, inefficient operation or even damage to your plumbing system. Professional installation ensures proper sizing, correct connections and adherence to local plumbing codes.

    Maintenance mainly involves periodically adding salt to salt-based systems, usually every 4 to 8 weeks depending on water usage and hardness. It's also good practice to schedule annual check-ups with a professional plumber to ensure the system is operating efficiently and to address any potential issues.

    Softeners remove hardness minerals using salt, while conditioners change how minerals behave to reduce scale. Softeners are more effective for very hard water.

    A water softener targets hard minerals like calcium and magnesium to prevent scale buildup. A water filter, on the other hand, is designed to remove impurities like chlorine, sediment and other contaminants to improve water taste and safety.
