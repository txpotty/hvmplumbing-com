    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Des Plaines

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Des Plaines, IL typically ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation. A basic residential PVB for an irrigation system is usually on the lower end, while a commercial-grade RPZ assembly costs more because of material and labor requirements.

    Most residential installs take two to four hours including testing. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, particularly if piping must be reconfigured.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This confirms the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also help with scheduling these annual tests.

    Constant dripping or discharge from an RPZ relief valve, low pressure downstream and a failed annual test are common signs. Visible corrosion or a cracked body from freezing also means service is needed.

    Most residential backflow preventer installations can be completed within 2 to 4 hours. More complex commercial systems or installations requiring significant pipe changes might take longer. We always aim to complete the work efficiently while keeping our quality standards high.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for new backflow assemblies, especially on commercial properties. We check local rules and pull the permit when needed.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice depends on the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.

    It depends on the hazard and local rules. Irrigation usually uses a PVB or double check, while high hazard connections like commercial kitchens or chemical injection require an RPZ.
