    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Kankakee

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the available water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the most effective ways to extend pump life.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Kankakee, IL

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are common causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to match your new tank's pre-charge.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Kankakee

    Most well pressure tank replacements cost $900 to $2,200 installed, depending on tank size and whether the switch and tank tee are replaced. Large capacity or multi-tank setups can run $2,500 or more. We give a written price before starting.

    Regular maintenance for a well pressure tank involves checking the air pressure in the tank at least once a year, ensuring it's properly pre-charged. You should also listen for excessive pump cycling and visually inspect the tank for any signs of corrosion or leaks. We can include this check during regular well system servicing.

    Common signs include your well pump cycling on and off very often (short-cycling), fluctuating water pressure at your faucets, no water coming from the tap, or a waterlogged tank when you tap on it. Visible rust or leaks around the tank are also obvious indicators.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, completely isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are generally preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    Many residential tanks carry 5 to 7 year manufacturer warranties. We add our own labor warranty on the installation.
