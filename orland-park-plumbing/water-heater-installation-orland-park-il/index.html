    Expansion Tank Installation in Orland Park, IL

    Many homes have a pressure reducing valve or check valve that creates a closed system. When water heats and expands, pressure can rise and stress pipes and relief valves. An expansion tank absorbs that extra pressure. We install and pre-charge the tank to match your water pressure. This protects your new water heater and pipes.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Orland Park

    The cost of water heater installation in Orland Park, IL can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any needed plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units generally have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your specific needs.

    Many tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, though hard water can shorten that. Regular flushing and anode rod checks help extend life.

    Rusty water, leaks around the base, rumbling noises and running out of hot water quickly are common signs. If your heater is over 10 years old, replacement is often the smart move.

    HVM Plumbing installs all major reputable brands of water heaters, including Rheem, Bradford White, A.O. Smith and Noritz. We work with both gas and electric models, as well as various sizes and efficiencies. Our team can help you choose the best brand and model to suit your hot water demands and budget.

    Most households of two to four people do well with a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by gallons per minute.

    To improve efficiency, ensure your new water heater is properly sized for your home, consider a tankless model for on-demand heating, insulate hot water pipes and set the thermostat to 120°F (49°C) to prevent scalding and cut energy consumption.

    The decision to repair or replace often depends on the age of your unit, the cost of the repair and its energy efficiency. If your water heater is over 10 years old, often breaks down, or the repair cost is more than 50% of a new unit, replacement is often the more economical choice. Newer models also offer much better energy efficiency.

    A traditional tank water heater typically lasts between 8 and 12 years with proper maintenance. Tankless water heaters often have a longer lifespan, ranging from 15 to 20 years, thanks to their durable components and on-demand operation.
