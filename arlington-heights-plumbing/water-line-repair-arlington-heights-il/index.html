    A homeowner can sometimes tighten a leaking fitting at an exposed hose bib. Buried service line repairs require locating, safe excavation, 811 utility marking and code-approved fittings. Mistakes can cause bigger leaks, so most homeowners call a licensed plumber.

    Preventing future water line issues involves a few key steps: being mindful of landscaping near buried lines, promptly addressing minor leaks, and considering a whole-home water pressure regulator if your pressure is consistently high. Regular plumbing inspections can also help identify potential problems before they grow. For older homes in Arlington Heights, proactive pipe replacement might be the best long-term solution.

    Water lines can leak or break due to several factors, including aging pipes (corrosion in galvanized steel or copper), shifting soil, tree root intrusion, high water pressure, ground freezing and thawing cycles, and accidental damage during excavation. Poor initial installation can also lead to early failure.

    Common signs of a broken water line include surprisingly high water bills, a sudden drop in water pressure, wet spots in your yard or on pavement that don't dry, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. For homes on slab foundations, look for cracks in the foundation or warm spots on the floor.

    Most standard water line repairs can be completed within a few hours to a full day, depending on the severity and location of the leak.
