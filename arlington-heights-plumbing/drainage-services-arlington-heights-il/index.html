    Kitchen, Bath and Laundry Drain Clearing in Arlington Heights

    Slow sinks, gurgling tubs and overflowing laundry standpipes typically come from grease, hair, soap scum and mineral scale. Hard water across much of Illinois leaves mineral scale in water heaters, faucets, and appliances, so softeners are a common upgrade. Hard water leaves calcium deposits that narrow pipes and catch debris faster than soft water does. We clear lines with the right size cable for each fixture and avoid harsh chemicals that damage pipes. If clogs keep coming back, we camera the line to find the reason.

    Channel and Trench Drains in Arlington Heights, IL

    Driveways and patios that slope toward the house can send storm runoff straight into a garage or under a door. A channel drain with a grate catches that sheet flow before it reaches the building. We use durable polymer or concrete channels from brands like NDS and set them flush with the surface. The channel ties into solid pipe that carries the water to a safe outlet.

    French Drain Installation in Arlington Heights

    A French drain is a gravel-filled trench with perforated pipe that collects water and carries it away from problem areas. It works well along foundations, behind retaining walls and in low spots that hold water after storms. We wrap the gravel in filter fabric so fine soil does not clog the system. The drain is sloped to an outlet, dry well or approved discharge point.

    Common signs include slow drains, gurgling sounds from pipes or toilets, unpleasant odors coming from drains, water backing up into sinks or showers, and multiple fixtures draining slowly at once. If you notice any of these, it's time to call a professional.

    A standard residential drain cleaning for a single fixture usually takes about one to two hours. More complex jobs, like main sewer line clearing or hydro-jetting, can take longer, potentially three to four hours or more depending on the severity of the blockage.

    For most homes, a preventive drain cleaning every one to two years is sufficient.     While easy to find, chemical drain cleaners are generally not recommended. They often contain harsh chemicals that can corrode pipes over time, especially older ones, and can be dangerous if not handled properly. Professional plumbers use safer, more effective methods.

    Minor drain cleaning usually does not require a permit.
