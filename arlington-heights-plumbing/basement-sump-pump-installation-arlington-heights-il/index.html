    New Sump Pit Excavation in Arlington Heights

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects.     Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Arlington Heights

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    Typically, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an extra premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    Illinois has a large stock of older homes, and many still have cast iron drains, galvanized steel supply lines, or original clay sewer pipe. Homes built on slabs without basements rarely need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water quickly during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    Backwater valves, overhead sewers, and battery backup sump pumps are popular upgrades for homes with a history of flooding. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is strongly recommended. It provides crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a smart investment for peace of mind.

    Replacing a pump generally does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they apply.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also warning signs.

    Plumbers in Illinois must hold a state license, so confirming a license before work starts is one of the easiest ways to protect your home. In many areas, a plumbing permit is required for new sump pump installations or significant replacements so the work meets local building codes. HVM Plumbing knows local permitting requirements in Arlington Heights and can help you obtain the necessary permits so your installation is compliant.
