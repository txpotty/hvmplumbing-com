    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Replacement in Peoria

    The cost of water heater replacement in Peoria, IL typically ranges from $1,200 to $2,500 for a standard tank unit, including installation. Tankless units can range from $3,000 to $5,000 or more, depending on the model and needed plumbing modifications. Factors like unit type, capacity, brand and labor complexity influence the final price.

    Generally, if your water heater is over 10 years old, frequently breaks down or has a major issue like a leaking tank, replacement is often more cost-effective in the long run. For newer units with minor, easily fixable issues, repair might be a viable option. Our technicians can provide an honest assessment.

    Manufacturer tank warranties typically range from 6 to 12 years depending on the model. We also warranty our installation labor.

    Flushing a traditional tank water heater annually is recommended in most homes. Homes supplied by lake water tend to have moderate water, while most of Illinois relies on groundwater that runs hard to very hard. This helps remove sediment buildup, which can reduce efficiency and shorten the lifespan of the unit. Regular flushing can also prevent scale from damaging heating elements.

    Most standard tank-style water heater replacements can be completed in 3 to 5 hours, assuming no major complications or plumbing reconfigurations. Tankless or hybrid installations might take a bit longer, often 5 to 8 hours, due to more complex venting or gas line adjustments.

    HVM Plumbing installs and services all major brands, including Bradford White, Rheem, AO Smith, Rinnai, Navien and State Water Heaters. We can help you choose the best brand and model that fits your budget and hot water demands.

    A traditional water heater stores and heats a limited amount of water in a tank, whereas a tankless water heater heats water on demand as it flows through the unit. Tankless units offer endless hot water and can be more energy-efficient, but usually have a higher upfront cost and may require gas line upgrades.

    Tank water heaters often last about 8 to 12 years, and hard water and sediment buildup can shorten that. Homes supplied by lake water tend to have moderately hard water, while most of Illinois relies on groundwater that runs hard to very hard. Annual flushing and anode rod checks can help extend that life.
