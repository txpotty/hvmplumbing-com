    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Peoria

    The cost of water heater installation in Peoria, IL can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any necessary plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units generally have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a written, upfront quote after assessing your specific needs.

    Rusty water, leaks around the base, rumbling noises and running out of hot water quickly are common signs. If your heater is over 10 years old, replacement is often the smart move.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, water heater installation involves working with gas lines, high voltage electricity and water connections, all of which carry serious risks if not handled correctly. Improper installation can lead to leaks, gas explosions, electrical fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. It can also void your warranty, and local codes typically require a licensed plumber for this work.

    The correct size water heater depends on the number of people in your household and your peak hot water demand. For tank units, a 40 to 50 gallon tank is common for 2 to 4 people, while larger families might need 60 to 80 gallons. For tankless units, it is about flow rate (GPM) and temperature rise. Our plumbers will assess your needs to recommend the ideal size.

    We install Rheem, A.O. Smith, Bradford White, Navien, Rinnai and other trusted brands. We recommend models based on reliability, warranty and budget.

    If your home has a pressure reducing valve or check valve, an expansion tank is usually required. It protects your water heater and pipes from pressure spikes.

    The decision to repair or replace often depends on the age of your unit, the cost of the repair and its energy efficiency. If your water heater is over 10 years old, frequently breaks down, or the repair cost is more than 50% of a new unit, replacement is often the more cost-effective choice. Newer models also offer much better energy efficiency.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for water heater replacement. We pull permits when required and make sure the install meets code.
