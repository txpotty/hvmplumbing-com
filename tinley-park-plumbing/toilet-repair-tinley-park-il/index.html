    Most flapper, fill valve and supply line repairs take 20 to 45 minutes. Pulling and resetting a toilet usually takes about an hour, and flange repairs can add another 30 to 60 minutes.

    To prevent future toilet problems, avoid flushing non-biodegradable items, use appropriate toilet paper, and address small issues like a running toilet promptly. Regular plumbing maintenance and annual inspections can also help catch potential problems before they escalate.

    Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium deposits on flush valve seats, fill valve ports and rim jets. Over time this causes leaks, slow refills and weak flushes. Descaling and choosing parts made to resist scale help repairs last longer.

    If your toilet is overflowing, right away reach behind the toilet and turn the small valve on the water supply line clockwise to shut off the water.     If the porcelain is sound and the toilet flushes well, a repair is generally the smart choice. If it uses 3.5 gallons or more per flush, clogs often or has a cracked tank, replacing it with a 1.28 gallon WaterSense model often makes more sense.

    Weak flushing can be caused by several issues, including insufficient water in the tank, a clogged rim jet, a partially closed stop valve, or a partial clog in the drain line. Our plumbers can diagnose the precise cause and restore full flushing power.
