    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Tinley Park

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which protects the motor. Water and sewer lines must be buried well below the frost line to avoid freezing, and shallow lines are a common source of winter trouble. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the line.

    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Tinley Park

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a solid fit for narrow pits in older homes. They typically last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we usually recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Tinley Park

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    Heavy spring and summer storms often overwhelm older sewer systems in Illinois, causing basement backups through floor drains. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is strongly recommended. It provides crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    Newer homes and remodels mostly use copper or PEX for supply lines, while older two-flats and bungalows often mix several materials. Homes built on slabs without basements seldom need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water quickly during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    Typically, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an additional premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    In many areas, including Tinley Park, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to ensure compliance with local building codes. HVM Plumbing will help you navigate the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained annually, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and confirming the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and lengthens the life of your pump.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is usually not allowed by local codes.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.
