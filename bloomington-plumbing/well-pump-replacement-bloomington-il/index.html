    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Bloomington

    Common signs include no water from your faucets, weak or inconsistent water pressure, strange noises from the well or pressure tank area, short cycling where the pump turns on and off frequently, or cloudy or muddy water. If you notice any of these, it is time to call a professional for an inspection.

    If your pressure tank is old or waterlogged, replacing it at the same time can protect the new pump. A failed tank causes rapid short cycling, which is one of the most common reasons submersible motors burn out early.

    The lifespan of a well pump varies widely depending on usage, water quality and pump type, but most last between 10 and 20 years. Regular maintenance and proper sizing can extend its life, but eventually wear and tear will call for replacement.

    Most well pumps last 8 to 15 years. Sediment, hard water and frequent cycling can cut lifespan.

    Common signs of a failing well pump include no water, sputtering faucets, reduced water pressure, discolored water or unusually high electricity bills. If your pump runs constantly or cycles on and off too frequently, these are also signs that replacement might be necessary.
