    The most common causes are a worn flapper, a mineral-crusted flush valve seat or a fill valve set too high so water spills into the overflow tube. A chain that is too short can also hold the flapper open. A quick inspection typically pinpoints the cause in minutes.

    A failed wax ring or a loose, cracked flange is the usual cause. Condensation on the tank can also drip down and look like a base leak. We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and reset it on a new seal.

    Most standard toilet repairs, such as fixing a running toilet or replacing internal tank components, can be completed within 1 to 2 hours. More complex issues like clearing a tough main line clog or replacing a wax ring might take longer, potentially 2 to 4 hours.

    Swapping a flapper is a reasonable DIY job if you match the right size and the flush valve seat is smooth. Base leaks, flange repairs and cracked tanks are better left to a plumber because a bad reset can cause hidden water damage to the subfloor.
