    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Glenview, IL

    For low-hazard applications, such as lawn irrigation systems in Glenview, IL, a pressure vacuum breaker, or PVB, is a common and cost-effective backflow prevention device. PVBs protect against back-siphonage by letting air into the system when pressure drops. Our certified plumbers carefully install PVBs above the highest point of downstream piping, as code requires, to maintain effective protection. We also make sure of proper clearance and drainage.

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Assembly Installation in Glenview

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of backflow protection and are often required for high-hazard applications in Glenview. These devices feature two independent check valves and a relief valve that opens to discharge water if pressure differentials indicate a backflow condition. HVM Plumbing focuses on precise RPZ installation, with proper sizing, placement and testing to meet all regulatory requirements for residential and commercial properties. We work with trusted brands like Watts and Febco.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Glenview

    Residential pressure vacuum breakers usually cost $250 to $700 installed. Double check and RPZ assemblies for larger homes or businesses range from $800 to $3,500 or more depending on size and enclosure needs.

    We regularly install high-quality backflow preventers from trusted brands such as Watts, Wilkins by Zurn, Apollo and Conbraco. Our plumbers are experienced with a wide range of devices and can recommend the best option for your application and budget.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and other equipment.

    It depends on the hazard and local rules. Irrigation often uses a PVB or double check, while high hazard connections like commercial kitchens or chemical injection require an RPZ.

    Backflow preventers are installed at cross-connection points where non-potable water could enter the potable water supply. Common locations include irrigation system connections, fire sprinkler lines, boiler systems and connections to auxiliary water supplies, often just after the main water meter or at the point of hazard.

    We install Watts, Febco, Wilkins by Zurn and Apollo assemblies. Each has easy to find repair kits, which keeps future service affordable.

    Constant dripping or discharge from an RPZ relief valve, low pressure downstream and a failed annual test are common signs. Visible corrosion or a cracked body from freezing also means service is needed.

    Installing a backflow preventer is a complex task that requires plumbing knowledge, tools and certification to meet local codes and properly protect your water supply. Improper installation can lead to ineffective protection, fines or health risks. It is always recommended to hire a licensed professional like HVM Plumbing.
