    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is built specifically to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve separate purposes.

    Pump replacements usually take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures. These symptoms point to a failing unit that requires immediate attention.

    A well-maintained sewage ejector pump typically has a lifespan of 7 to 10 years, though some higher-quality units can last longer. Factors like frequency of use, quality of installation and the type of waste being pumped can affect its longevity. Regular maintenance can help lengthen its operating life.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can affect its lifespan. Annual inspections can help extend its longevity.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional inspect your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.
