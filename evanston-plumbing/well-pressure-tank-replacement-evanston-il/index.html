    Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in Evanston

    When your well pressure tank shows signs of trouble, it is important to determine if a repair is practical or if full replacement is necessary. Small air leaks or issues with the air valve might be repairable. However, if the tank itself is corroded, has a ruptured diaphragm, or is waterlogged, replacement is almost always the more cost-effective and reliable option. Our technicians in Evanston provide an honest assessment, explaining the pros and cons of repair versus a complete well pressure tank replacement, so you can make an informed decision.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Evanston, IL

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are frequent causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to match your new tank's pre-charge.

    Preventative Well Pressure Tank Maintenance in Evanston, IL

    While our primary service is well pressure tank replacement, HVM Plumbing also stresses the importance of preventative maintenance for your well system in Evanston, IL. Regular checks of your pressure tank's air charge and overall condition can help identify potential issues before they lead to a complete failure. During a maintenance visit, we can top off the air charge, inspect for early signs of corrosion or leaks, and advise on steps to extend the lifespan of your current tank, potentially delaying the need for replacement.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Evanston, IL

    Diaphragm tanks like the Amtrol Well-X-Trol use a fixed membrane that separates air above from water below. They are durable, but the steel shell can rust through at the base after years of condensation in a hot, damp equipment space. We replace corroded diaphragm tanks and check the stand and fittings under them. The new tank is leveled and supported so it does not strain the piping. This restores steady pressure to every faucet.
