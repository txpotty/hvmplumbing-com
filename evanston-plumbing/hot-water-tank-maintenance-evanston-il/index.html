    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Evanston, IL

    The anode rod, often called the sacrificial rod, is essential for preventing corrosion inside your hot water tank. Water chemistry varies from one neighborhood to the next, and it changes the rod's lifespan. Our technicians inspect the anode rod for wear and recommend replacement if it's badly corroded, typically every 3 to 5 years. Replacing a worn anode rod is a cost-effective way to protect your tank from rust and leaks, saving you from a full tank replacement.

    Standard Hot Water Tank Flushing in Evanston

    Sediment buildup is a common issue in hot water tanks, especially where water hardness runs high. Our standard flushing service removes these mineral deposits, which can reduce efficiency and lead to premature tank failure. We drain the tank completely, flush out all sediment and refill it, giving you clear water flow and improved heating performance. This routine service is key for extending the life of your unit.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Evanston

    A standard flush and inspection typically runs $125 to $250 depending on tank size and access. Anode replacement typically adds $75 to $200 in parts and labor, and powered anodes cost more.

    A typical hot water tank maintenance service generally takes about 1 to 2 hours. This includes draining, flushing, inspection and any minor adjustments or part swaps like an anode rod.

    That sound is water trapped under a layer of sediment boiling as the burner fires. A flush often quiets it, though very old hardened scale may not come out completely.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which counts most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it extends the lifespan of your unit.

    Once a year is the minimum for most homes, and more often where water is hard. Homes without a softener or with heavy use may do better with a flush every six months.

    Most flushes and inspections take 45 minutes to 90 minutes. Heavily scaled tanks or anode replacements in tight closets can take a bit longer.

    That smell usually comes from bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. We can switch to an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank to get rid of it.
