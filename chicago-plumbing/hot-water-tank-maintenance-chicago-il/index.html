    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Chicago

    The temperature and pressure relief valve is the part that keeps a tank from becoming dangerous. We lift the lever, confirm it opens and reseats and check the discharge line for proper material and termination. We also check your thermal expansion tank pressure with a gauge, since a waterlogged expansion tank lets pressure jump every heating cycle. High street pressure is common in many service areas, so we test static pressure at a hose bib too.

    Electric Element and Thermostat Service in Chicago

    Electric tanks rely on upper and lower heating elements that get crusted with scale in hard water. We test each element with a meter, check the thermostats and look for scorched wires at the terminal block. If an element has failed we replace it with the matching wattage and voltage. Plenty of families who think they need a new heater just need a lower element and a flush.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Chicago

    A standard flush and inspection usually runs $125 to $250 depending on tank size and access. Anode replacement typically adds $75 to $200 in parts and labor, and powered anodes cost more.

    Once a year is the minimum for most homes, and more often where water is hard. Homes without a softener or with heavy use may do better with a flush every six months.

    Routine hot water tank maintenance, such as flushing and inspections, typically does not require a permit in Chicago. However, if major components are replaced or the entire unit is swapped out, a plumbing permit may be necessary. Our team handles all necessary permit applications.

    Common signs include reduced hot water supply, strange noises like rumbling or popping, rust-colored water, a sulfur or rotten egg smell, or water leaking from the tank. These indicate issues that maintenance can often resolve.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual hot water tank maintenance. This is particularly true where water is hard, since minerals speed up sediment buildup and anode rod depletion.

    In mineral-heavy water a magnesium rod may last three to five years. We measure it at every visit so you replace it before the tank lining starts to rust.

    While some basic steps like draining a small amount of water can be done by a homeowner, professional hot water tank maintenance involves specialized tools and experience to properly inspect electrical or gas components, anode rods and pressure relief valves. It is best left to a professional to ensure safety and code compliance.

    Many homeowners can do a basic drain with a garden hose. The risk is a plastic drain valve that breaks or will not reseal, and DIY flushes seldom include an anode check or relief valve test.
