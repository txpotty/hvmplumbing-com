    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Chicago

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules permit. We size them to avoid too much pressure loss on your system.

    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Chicago, IL

    For low-hazard applications, such as lawn irrigation systems in Chicago, IL, a pressure vacuum breaker, or PVB, is a popular and cost-effective backflow prevention device. PVBs protect against back-siphonage by letting air into the system when pressure drops. Our certified plumbers carefully install PVBs above the highest point of downstream piping, as code requires, to maintain effective protection. We also check for proper clearance and drainage.

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Device Installation in Chicago, IL

    For high-hazard applications in Chicago, IL where toxic substances could contaminate the water supply, a reduced pressure zone, or RPZ, device is often required. RPZ backflow preventers offer the highest level of protection, using a relief valve to discharge water if the pressure drops. HVM Plumbing specializes in RPZ installation, making sure all components are correctly sized, positioned and set up according to state and local plumbing codes.     Most residential backflow preventer installations can be finished within 2 to 4 hours. More complex commercial installations or those requiring extensive pipe changes might take a full day. We always aim for prompt service with minimal disruption to your water supply.

    Backflow preventers are usually installed as a preventive measure or because code requires them. Signs of possible cross-contamination include discolored or foul-smelling tap water, unexplained drops in water pressure or visible dirty water entering a clean water source, though these often point to broader plumbing issues as well.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice depends on the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.
